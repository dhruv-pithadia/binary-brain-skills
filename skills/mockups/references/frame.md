# Frame

Aim: get the problem right before designing the answer. Mockups built on a misread need fail however polished they are.

## Ask

Use the available user-question tool or ask directly in chat, up to four questions per round, as many rounds as the answers are worth. Skip anything the project already answers. Every question must change what you would build. Give concrete options with your recommendation first.

Question bank (a menu, not a script):

- Who uses this, how often, and in what moment (rushed, focused, on a phone, mid-task)?
- What are they trying to get done, and what goes wrong for them if this fails?
- How much data: one item, fifty, ten thousand? Real examples of values?
- What do they do today instead? What do they complain about?
- Constraints: API limits, permissions, compliance, performance, other teams?
- What does success look like, and how would you know?
- What have you already imagined, or seen elsewhere and liked or disliked?
- Greenfield only: who is the audience, what should it feel like, which products' feel do you admire?

If the request looks like the wrong solution to the real need (a settings page where a smart default would do), say so in one sentence and carry the alternative into Diverge as an option.

## Brief

Write at most ten lines, then ask the user to confirm or correct:

- **Job:** who does what, in which moment, and why it matters.
- **Scenarios:** happy path, empty or first-use, worst case.
- **Success signal.**
- **MATCH / OPEN / IDENTITY** from Ground.
- **Assumptions:** numbered. Mark the ones that would change the structure if wrong; only those need a reply.

Do not proceed on an unconfirmed structure-changing assumption when a user is present.
